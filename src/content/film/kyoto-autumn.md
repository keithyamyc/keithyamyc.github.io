I went to Kyoto in November expecting red. I got grey, and then red, and then grey again.

The maples turned a week later than the forecasts said, which meant that for the first four days I was photographing the wrong thing — or rather, I was photographing what was there instead of what I had come for. In retrospect those four days produced the better frames.

## On photographing a place everyone has photographed

Kyoto is possibly the most photographed city in Japan, which is saying something. Every composition you can make has been made, at a better time of day, by someone with a larger camera.

The only way through this is to stop trying to make the *good* photograph and start making the *specific* one. What is the light doing at 6:40 that it is not doing at 10? What is the sound of a place where nobody is talking?

## Technical notes

Portra 400 at box speed, metered for the shadows and developed normally. Three frames were underexposed by a stop because I forgot to reset the ISO after a roll of HP5 — those turned out to be the ones I like most, which is not a lesson.

The 35mm Summicron is the only lens I brought. I have never regretted travelling with one lens, and I regret it slightly less each year.

## What I did not photograph

- The inside of a temple where a woman was praying. It would have been a beautiful photograph and it was not mine to take.
- My own breakfast, which was the same every day and which I will remember for the rest of my life.
- The rain, which does not photograph well and does not need to.

## Afterword

Four rolls, four hundred and forty-four kilometres walked, one cold that lasted the whole trip.

I would go back tomorrow.
