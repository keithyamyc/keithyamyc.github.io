I shoot film three or four times a year. Not because it is better. Because it is slower, and I need the slowness.

People ask the same six questions. Here are the answers.

## 1. Why not digital?

I do shoot digital. Almost everything on this website that is not in the film section was taken digitally, because I am not a purist and I do not want to be.

Film is a different kind of attention. Thirty-six frames means you have to decide. The decision is the point.

## 2. Is it expensive?

Yes. Roughly two dollars a frame once you include developing and scanning, and that is with a cheap lab. It is a hobby with a subscription fee.

This is also useful. When every frame costs money, you stop taking photographs of nothing.

## 3. What camera?

A Leica M6 and a Rolleiflex 3.5, both older than I am. Neither is necessary. A Pentax K1000 would do 90% of the work for 5% of the price.

The camera matters less than the fact that you carry it.

## 4. What film?

- **Kodak Portra 400** — for people, and for anything where you want the colours to be kind.
- **Ilford HP5 Plus** — for cities, and for weather.
- **Fujifilm Provia 100F** — for when the light is doing something and you want it recorded exactly.

I do not use anything exotic. Exotic film is a way of avoiding the actual work.

## 5. How do you get the colours like that?

I do not. The lab scans it, I adjust the contrast slightly, and that is all. Most of what people respond to in film photographs is not the film — it is the light, and the fact that the photographer waited.

## 6. Do you develop it yourself?

No. I have tried. I am bad at it, and I dislike the chemicals. Paying someone else twelve dollars to do it properly is the best deal in photography.

---

The honest answer to all six questions is the same: it is a hobby, it is not important, and it makes me look at things more carefully than I otherwise would.

That last part is the whole reason.
